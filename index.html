2024

Why now: India drew a line on green claims.

In 2024, India's Central Consumer Protection Authority issued guidelines saying environmental claims must be specific and backed by evidence. The rules exist. What's missing is a quick way for shoppers to see which claims meet them. That's Asli.

A claims checker has to play by stricter rules than the claims.

So we built Asli. It means real, and that's the only test it runs.

Paste a link for a detergent, shampoo, body wash, handwash, dishwash or floor cleaner. Asli sorts its green claims into two piles. Claims that are specific and backed by something you can check. Claims too vague to mean anything.

Then it compares cost per wash, not price per bottle, and looks for a switch that uses less plastic per use without costing you more.

What it won't do
No single eco score, because one number hides too much.
No brand pays to rank higher.
Every finding shows how confident we are. If the evidence is thin, Asli tells you to keep what you already use.

One honest caveat. Less plastic isn't automatically better. Some refill pouches are harder to recycle than the bottle they replace, so Asli flags that instead of burying it.

Fair questions

Is Asli another eco-rating app?

No. Rating apps give a single score that hides how little is actually known. Asli shows each claim separately, says how confident it is, and gives you one practical answer: switch, or keep what you have.

Which products does it cover?

We're starting with six everyday categories where refills, concentrates and bigger packs make a real difference: laundry detergent, dishwash, floor cleaner, hand wash, body wash and shampoo.

Do brands pay you?

No brand can pay for a placement or a better result. If we ever earn a commission on a switch, the verdict is decided first and the commission is disclosed on the card.

Is a "vague" claim the same as a false one?

No. Vague means the claim doesn't say what it's based on. We describe the claim and its evidence. We never accuse a brand of bad intent.
